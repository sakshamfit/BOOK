## Visual language

Warm paper, near-black olive ink, vermilion accents, earthy green editorial stages, and saturated books. Large restrained sans-serif titles meet literary italic serif phrases. The covers supply the color; the interface provides quiet space. Local Nimbus Sans files are used for consistent rendering, with a system literary serif fallback.

## Original image-generation prompt

Mode: built-in image-generation tool. Asset: one eight-cover publishing atlas, subsequently sliced into eight separate optimized WebP covers. Original output was 1448 × 1086; each cover is approximately 362 × 543. No 4K resolution is claimed.

Prompt: Create a single production book-cover atlas for the fictional premium independent bookstore FOLIO. Four columns by two rows, no gutters or margins, equal flat front covers with 2:3 individual aspect ratios. Sophisticated Swiss typography, tactile paper print, clear large titles. Top row: THE ART OF NOTICING by ELLA MARLOW, ivory with gestural black line and cobalt circle; FORMS OF SILENCE by NOAH VALE, ultramarine with elegant white typography and a pale sculptural stone; A FIELD GUIDE TO WONDER by IRIS WREN, chartreuse with dark botanical fern; THE WORLD IN BETWEEN by MIRA SOL, lavender with a red sun and surreal terraced architecture. Bottom row: AFTER THE LAST TRAIN by LEO ASH, vermilion with blue night-train window; THE SHAPE OF EVERYDAY by ADA REED, near-black with large cream geometric Bauhaus forms; WILD ATLAS by JUNE ALDER, mint and forest-green photogram foliage; SMALL HOURS by EMIL NORTH, pale yellow with a moon, thin black botanical lines, and a lone blue window. No real publisher trademarks or endorsement claims. Flat artwork only, no mockup perspective.

Final integrated assets: `public/covers/book-1.webp` through `public/covers/book-8.webp`.

## Pop-up reference — second iteration

`WhatsApp Video 2026-10-29 at 4.01.54 PM.mp4` — 29.39 seconds, 816 × 480, 60 fps. Inspected as sequential contact sheets and enlarged crops.

The reference opens a yellow physical book. The left page supplies concise explanation, while a mechanism rises from the right page: a ball on a curved track, colored energy stores, heat and molecules, raised blocks, a turbine, and a staircase. Cut-paper outlines, thick page edges, small labels, moving color, and closer camera views make abstract ideas spatial and causal. The central interaction is change → visible consequence. A page is a working model, rather than an illustration beside prose.

Folio translates that interaction pattern into eight book-specific worlds. Each book opens a cover over a three-dimensional paper spread, with the explanation printed on the left and a raised scene on the right. The guided sequence changes both the text and the model. Color choices track object roles. Object picking and equivalent labeled HTML buttons select the same concepts. Sliders, switches, play/pause, camera orbit, zoom, and a closer view provide several levels of exploration. Each world has a standalone route and retains access to the sample and author.

The original film's mechanics are not copied as scientific claims into the fictional literary catalog. The fern is explicitly a symbolic paper-growth model; the atlas is an imagined landscape. Each scene expresses the actual original sample's theme: attention, negative space, curiosity, choice, connection, design, discovery, or poetry.

3D models and diagrams are code-native Three.js geometry. WebGL failure keeps the text and controls and substitutes a corresponding SVG illustration. No additional raster images were needed.
